The premise

Price is visible.
Value takes work.

The database brings performance, trajectory and cost into one decision surface—so a scout can move from thousands of players to a defensible shortlist.

Rather than hide the model behind one black-box ranking, every major lens can be explored separately: form, potential, overall quality and value.

One model. Six workflows.

From first scan to a simulated season, each view answers a distinct football decision.

01 / Player value

See who is underpriced

Compare performance against wages and market value, then filter by league, club, age and position.

02 / Team value

Separate resources from results

Assess clubs through league-adjusted performance and squad expectations rather than reputation alone.

03 / Manager value

Measure impact from the touchline

Explore recent form, career overperformance, trajectory and value relative to manager compensation.

04 / Doppelgängers

Find the next-best fit

Match players by a position-specific statistical profile to identify stylistically similar alternatives.

05 / Squad suggestions

Turn a need into a shortlist

Translate positional gaps and constraints into targeted recommendations from the full player pool.

06 / Squad builder

Test the team, not just the transfer

Construct an XI, simulate a domestic season and follow the same squad through European competition.

Methodology you can challenge.

A useful model should make its judgments inspectable. The product identifies what is measured, how scores behave and where the data has limits.

01

Position-aware analysis

Players are assessed against the demands and statistical profile of their role, not one universal box-score standard.

02

Time horizons stay distinct

Longer-run performance estimates true quality; last-10 form remains a visible hot-or-cold signal rather than quietly rewriting the model.

03

Context travels with the score

League strength, minutes, sample size and data coverage are surfaced so a ranking can be interpreted—not merely repeated.

04

Estimates are labeled

Projected wages and other modeled inputs are distinguished from reported figures, with limitations documented in the interface.

Design principleA clear limitation builds more trust than false precision.
